This month's spotlight

Burlington, Vermont

A lake town with a big heart and an even bigger maple habit. This month's Marlowby box is packed with syrup, cheddar and handmade goods from the small businesses of Vermont's Queen City.

Inside the box

Eight tastes of the Queen City

Basic boxes get five of these. Premium boxes get all eight. Every item comes from a Burlington-area maker with a story card that tells you who made it.

  • In every box

    Pure maple syrup

    Grade A amber, boiled down over a wood fire at a Green Mountain sugarhouse.

  • In every box

    Maple cream candies

    Little maple leaves made from nothing but syrup, cooked and stirred by hand.

  • In every box

    Small-batch dark chocolate

    Bean-to-bar chocolate from a tiny kitchen a few blocks from the lake, wrapped in local art.

  • In every box

    Sharp cheddar crackers

    Baked with aged Vermont cheddar for a snap that tastes like a farm stand.

  • In every box

    Letterpress lake postcard

    A sunset over the Adirondacks, printed by hand on a vintage press.

  • Premium only

    Hand-thrown stoneware mug

    Glazed the blue-grey of Lake Champlain in winter by a potter with a lakeside studio.

  • Premium only

    Wool-blend trail socks

    Warm, tough socks made for a fall hike up into the Green Mountains.

  • Premium only

    Apple cider donut mix

    Fall in Vermont in a bag. Just add cider, a skillet and a cold morning.

Spend a day in Burlington

From the farmers market to the festival stage

Every item in the box comes from somewhere in this town. Here is a day that walks you past all of them.

  1. 8 AM

    Church Street Marketplace

    Four car-free blocks of local shops, cafés and street performers. Browse the shelves at Crow Bookshop, then grab a coffee and people-watch.

    In your box: the dark chocolate

  2. 10 AM

    The weekend farmers market

    Vermont growers, bakers and cheesemakers set up downtown with bread, apples and more cheddar than you can carry.

    In your box: the cheddar crackers

  3. 1 PM

    The Island Line Trail

    A bike path that hugs the lake and then runs out onto a long marble causeway, right into the middle of Lake Champlain.

    In your box: the trail socks, if you go Premium

  4. 4 PM

    The South End on Pine Street

    Peek into the kitchen at Lake Champlain Chocolates' flagship store, then head to Citizen Cider nearby in the South End.

    In your box: the maple cream candies

  5. 6 PM

    Sunset at Waterfront Park

    The sun drops behind the Adirondacks across the water and the whole sky goes orange. Grab a crêpe from The Skinny Pancake and find a bench.

    In your box: the letterpress postcard

  6. 9 PM

    Jazz after dark

    Music spills out of clubs and onto Church Street, and Leunig's is a favorite spot for a late set. Every June the Discover Jazz Festival takes over the whole city.

    Play the festival below

Real Burlington favorites

The small businesses that make the Queen City

These are real local shops, kitchens and makers worth a visit when you are in town. They are the spirit we pack into every Burlington box.

  • Chocolatier · Pine Street

    Lake Champlain Chocolates

    Making chocolate in Burlington since 1983. At the flagship store on Pine Street you can peek into the kitchen where the treats are made.

  • Cidery · South End

    Citizen Cider

    A South End cidery that presses apples from regional farms into hard cider.

  • Crêperie · near the waterfront

    The Skinny Pancake

    A crêpe spot that started right here in Burlington in 2003 and serves local food whenever it can.

  • Bookstore · Church Street

    Crow Bookshop

    An independent bookstore on the Marketplace with shelves of new, used and bargain books.

  • Bistro · Church Street

    Leunig's Bistro & Café

    A Paris-style bistro on the corner of Church and College Streets, and a favorite place to catch live jazz.

  • Fry stand · South Burlington

    Al's French Frys

    A roadside classic that has been serving fries to Burlington since 1946.

Did you know?

Ben & Jerry's scooped its very first cone in Burlington in 1978, inside an old gas station at the corner of St. Paul and College Streets.

    Your festival field guide

    • When

      Early June

      Five days of shows in early June, from lunchtime sets to late-night jams.

    • Where

      All over downtown

      The Flynn Main Stage, clubs and restaurants downtown, and free stages along the Church Street Marketplace.

    • Who

      Everyone

      Big touring names, Vermont musicians and local student jazz bands, sometimes on the same block.

    • Cost

      Plenty is free

      Bring a blanket. Many outdoor sets cost nothing, so you can wander from stage to stage.
