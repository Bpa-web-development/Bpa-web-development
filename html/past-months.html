January 2026

Savannah, Georgia

We started the year somewhere warm. Savannah's historic district is laid out around 22 leafy squares shaded by live oaks and Spanish moss, and its kitchens run on pecans, peaches and slow afternoons.

  • Pecan pralinesCooked in copper pots by a family candy kitchen
  • Georgia peach jamSmall-batch and barely sweetened
  • Lowcountry hot sauceAged peppers from a two-person kitchen
  • Live oak soy candleHand-poured, smells like a porch after rain
  • Hand-printed tea towelPremium only, with a block-print of the squares

22historic squares in the old city

February 2026

Providence, Rhode Island

A small capital with a big creative streak. Providence was once known for making jewelry, its art school keeps the streets full of makers, and on WaterFire nights bonfires burn right on the rivers downtown.

  • Coffee syrupStir it into milk for Rhode Island's official state drink
  • Sea salt caramelsFinished with salt from the Atlantic coast
  • Hand-stamped brass braceletFrom a jeweler in an old mill building
  • Riverfront art printScreen-printed by a local design student
  • Wool knit beaniePremium only, for a New England February

Coffee milkis Rhode Island's official state drink

March 2026

Lexington, Kentucky

Spring came early in the Horse Capital of the World. Lexington sits in the middle of the Bluegrass region, with rolling pastures, wooden fences, banjo music and farm kitchens that never skip dessert.

  • Sorghum caramelsMade with syrup pressed on a family farm
  • Smoked sea saltSlow-smoked over oak by a two-person spice shop
  • Bluegrass playlist cardPicked by a local fiddle teacher
  • Horse country postcard setWatercolors of the pastures in spring
  • Hand-thrown pottery bowlPremium only, glazed in bluegrass green

Horse Capitalof the World is Lexington's nickname
